# Deal structures a self-funded searcher actually sees

Each row: what it is, when a seller agrees to it, and how it sits with an SBA 7(a) lender. [Sources: Walker Deibel, *Buy Then Build* (the SBA-financed acquisition playbook); Ruback & Yudkoff, *HBR Guide to Buying a Small Business* (seller notes and earn-outs); Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference* (negotiating the note).]

| Structure | What it is | Seller's reason to accept | SBA treatment (see sba-rules.md) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-standby seller note | Seller carries part of the price; no principal or interest paid until the SBA loan is repaid (typically 10 years) | Gets a higher headline price; shows confidence in the business; often the only way the deal closes | Counts toward the 10% injection up to half of it. No payments → nothing in DSCR |
| Subordinated amortising seller note | Seller carries part of the price, paid monthly behind the bank | Income stream, interest, higher price | Counts in DSCR at ≤10-year amortisation. No injection credit |
| Interest-only then amortising ("partial standby") | Interest for 1–3 years, then principal | Seller gets something now; buyer gets breathing room | Same as above — now counted in DSCR, so the breathing room is on paper only |
| Seller rollover equity | Seller keeps a minority stake | Upside; continuity; tax deferral | Partial change of ownership: seller and every equity holder guarantee the loan for two years. Many sellers refuse once they learn this |
| Consulting / transition agreement | Paid role for the seller after close | Income, purpose, a graceful exit | Operating expense; keep it reasonable or the lender adds it back |
| Non-compete payment | Separate allocation of price | Tax treatment differs for seller | Part of total project cost |
| Earn-out (price contingent on future results) | Portion of price paid if targets are hit | Bridges a valuation gap | Historically not financeable with 7(a) proceeds — verify current SOP; usually structured outside the SBA loan |
| Performance-linked forgiveness | Seller note forgiven if revenue drops | Protects buyer from a customer walking | Outside SBA structures in practice; a lender will not count a note that may vanish |
| Minority investor equity | Friends, family, search investors with <20% and no control | Deal gets done | Now a Limited source of injection (SOP 50 10 8.1 — verify); no personal guarantee if under 20% in a complete change |
| 100% seller-financed | No bank | Seller wants the income stream and a fast close; buyer lacks SBA eligibility | No SBA involvement; the seller is the lender, so expect bank-like diligence |
| ROBS (401k rollover) | Buyer's retirement funds buy stock in a C-corp that buys the business | Buyer's own capital; no debt on that portion | Counts as unborrowed equity; needs a specialist provider and a C-corp |
| Conventional bank loan | Non-SBA term loan | Larger, cleaner businesses | Lender's own rules; usually more equity, shorter term |

## The negotiation, briefly [Voss]
- Label the seller's fear before it's spoken: "It seems like the thing you're worried about is being paid late."
- Calibrated questions do the work: "How would a note on standby for ten years need to look for you to feel safe?"
- Anchor the structure, not just the price: a higher headline with a standby note often beats a lower all-cash number for both sides.
- No deal is better than a bad deal. The DSCR line in the calculator is the searcher's walk-away number; it is not negotiable with the seller because it isn't the seller's to negotiate.
