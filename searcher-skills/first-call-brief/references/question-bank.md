# Question bank — first conversation with an owner

Built on two sources the searcher should read anyway:
- Neil Rackham, *SPIN Selling* — the order matters: Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff. Big-ticket conversations are won by questions, not pitches.
- Richard Ruback & Royce Yudkoff, *HBR Guide to Buying a Small Business* — the screening call is about three things: is it a good business, is the owner really a seller, and could you run it.

## Situation (facts, kept short — you already have the public ones)
- How did the business start, and what's changed most since?
- What does a normal week look like for you right now?
- Who's on the team, and how long have they been with you?
- Who are your biggest customers, and roughly what share of revenue is the top three?
- Do you own the building, or lease? How long is left?

## Problem (what's hard about the current setup)
- What would break first if you took a month off?
- Which part of the job do you still do yourself that you wish you didn't?
- What's the one thing you've been meaning to fix for years?

## Implication (why it matters — let them say it)
- If nothing changes, where is the business in five years?
- Has anyone approached you about buying? What happened?
- What would need to be true for you to feel good handing it to someone?

## Need-payoff (the picture of a good outcome, in their words)
- If this went exactly right, what does your life look like two years from now?
- What matters more to you: the number, the speed, or what happens to the people?
- Would you want to stay involved for a while, and in what role?

## Diligence facts to get before the second call (ask lightly, confirm later)
- Three years of tax returns and a current-year P&L
- Whose name the trade licence is in (if the business needs one)
- Any seller note appetite — "would you consider carrying part of the price?"
- Any family members in the business, and their expectations

## Listening cues
- "I've thought about it" = passive looking. "I talked to a broker" = active. "I turned down an offer" = has a number in mind; find out what it was.
- Staff tenure in decades + no family in the business = the classic handover candidate.
- Owner answers every question with "I" = key-person risk; price it, don't flinch at it.
